Why Mauritius is a good idea ♥

Easy to get in

No visa for us, 90 days on arrival.

Easy to stay

The Premium Visa is free, lasts a year and renews, as long as we have remote income.

Calm and stable

Known for safety and stability, which is the whole point.

Far from everything

An island in the middle of the Indian Ocean, far from every front line on your list.

Good doctors

Modern private clinics at prices that won't scare us.

We'd manage

English and French are everywhere, plus Kreol to learn.

Always warm

Beaches, lagoons, green mountains. Also humid, so you get to complain about the weather.

Home is a call away

Seven hours ahead of Brazil, so morning there is a fine time to call your mom.

Keyla's ready-to-fly kit

  • The slow part: a rabies blood test (FAVN or RFFIT) at least 4 weeks after her rabies shot. She can fly from 3 to 12 months after the blood draw, so we redo it every year and she stays ready.
  • ISO microchip, scanned by the vet when the blood is drawn.
  • Spayed, plus distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis and parvo shots.
  • Close to the trip: an Ehrlichia test within 45 days, a Brucella test, and a vet health certificate.
  • She travels as manifest cargo (never in the cabin), a Mauritius pet broker meets her, then at least 5 days of quarantine at Réduit.
  • She will protest the crate. It is what it is, so we make it SUPER comfy: pink, a pillow, her toys.
  • Her papers say SRD (mixed breed). A dog DNA test settles it if Mauritius asks, since mixes of some breeds (Rottweiler included) aren't allowed.

If red hits before her permit is in hand: all three of us go to the farm and fly together when it lands.
